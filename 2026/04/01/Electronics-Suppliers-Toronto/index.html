Where to Buy Electronics Components in Toronto

Published Apr 1, 2026
#Toronto #Electronics #Components #Hardware #FPGA #Hobby
◆

Whether you need a handful of resistors for a weekend project, an FPGA dev board for a deep dive into digital design, or just want to browse bins of components in person, Toronto and the GTA have you covered. Here is a rundown of the best places to buy electronic components locally and online in Canada.

Local Brick-and-Mortar Stores

Nothing beats walking into a store and leaving with parts in hand. These are the go-to spots in the GTA:

Creatron

Locations: College St (west of Spadina) and Birchmount & Sheppard

Creatron is probably the most well-known hobbyist electronics shop in downtown Toronto. They carry resistors, capacitors, transistors, op amps, Arduino and Raspberry Pi boards, sensors, LEDs, wire, tools, and more. You pay a bit of a premium for the convenience of walking in and grabbing exactly what you need, but for small quantities and quick prototyping it is hard to beat. Great for students.

Sayal Electronics

Location: Matheson Blvd, Mississauga

Sayal is a staple of the GTA electronics scene. They stock a massive selection of components: capacitors, resistors, ICs, Arduino kits, test equipment, connectors, enclosures, wire, solder, and hobby supplies. The Matheson Blvd location in Mississauga also has a cluster of nearby electronics stores (like AA Electronics) if you want to make a full trip of it. Pricing is a bit higher than ordering online but the in-person selection is impressive.

A1 Electronic Parts

Location: Near Sherway Gardens (Etobicoke)

A1 Parts has been serving the Toronto area for over 40 years. They carry electronic components, surplus parts, and computer equipment. Good prices, especially compared to the downtown shops. Worth the trip if you are in the west end.

Home Hardware (Queen St, Downtown)

Here is a hidden gem: the basement of the Home Hardware on Queen Street in downtown Toronto has bins of electronic parts. You pick out what you need, write it down on a piece of paper, and bring it upstairs to the cashier. It is a charmingly old-school experience and surprisingly useful for grabbing a few resistors or capacitors in a pinch.

Electronic Surplus Industries

Location: 53 Sheffield Road (near Caledonia/Castlefield)

If you enjoy digging through surplus, this is your place. They buy out labs that are shutting down and sell the equipment: old oscilloscopes, power supplies, signal generators, labware, and miscellaneous components. You never know what you will find. Not the place for buying a specific transistor, but great for cheap test equipment and serendipitous discoveries.

Canadian Online Stores

For larger orders, specific parts, or when you do not want to make the trek, these Canadian online stores ship quickly and save you from cross-border duties:

Lee's Electronic Components

Based in Vancouver but ships across Canada. Huge catalogue: resistors, capacitors (electrolytic, SMD, ceramic, variable), diodes, relays, switches, MOSFETs, ICs, connectors, and much more. Good prices and reliable shipping.

Universal Solder

A popular Canadian electronics parts store for professionals, students, and hobbyists. They carry kits, breakout boards, dev boards, components, and tools. Fast domestic shipping.

PiShop.ca

Canadian online store with a good selection of Raspberry Pi products, electronic components, sensors, and maker supplies.

Major International Distributors

When you need a specific part number, a datasheet-grade component, or are ordering in quantity, the big distributors are the way to go. Be mindful of shipping costs on small orders.

DigiKey

The gold standard for electronic component distribution. Millions of parts in stock, excellent parametric search, datasheets for everything, and fast shipping. They carry everything from basic passives to FPGAs (Xilinx/AMD, Intel/Altera, Lattice). Shipping to Canada is reasonable, and orders over a certain threshold often qualify for free shipping.

Mouser Electronics

Very similar to DigiKey in scope and quality. Massive inventory, great search tools, and good Canadian shipping options. Some people prefer Mouser's interface; others prefer DigiKey. Worth checking both for pricing and availability.

Newark Electronics Canada

Newark (element14/Farnell) is another major distributor with a wide selection and Canadian-specific site. Good for industrial and professional components.

Tayda Electronics

Extremely cheap components with international shipping via DHL, UPS, and FedEx. Great for stocking up on basic passives (resistors, caps, diodes) in bulk. The per-unit prices are hard to beat.

Tips

  • Small orders: Go local. Shipping costs from DigiKey/Mouser on a $5 order of resistors will hurt. Walk into Creatron or Sayal instead.
  • Specific part numbers: Use DigiKey or Mouser. Their parametric search is unmatched and they will have the exact manufacturer part you need.
  • FPGA dev boards: DigiKey and Mouser stock boards from AMD/Xilinx, Intel/Altera, and Lattice. For budget options, check out AliExpress for Tang Nano or similar boards, but expect longer shipping times.
  • Surplus and test equipment: Check out Electronic Surplus Industries or keep an eye on local Kijiji/Facebook Marketplace listings. Labs and universities periodically offload equipment.
  • Arduino/Raspberry Pi ecosystem: Creatron, PiShop.ca, and Universal Solder all have good selections.
